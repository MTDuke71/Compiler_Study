# Week 04 - 2026

**Week:** January 19 - January 25, 2026

## Overview

Week 4 focuses on **Lexical Analysis** - the first compiler phase. Monday through Friday covers building a working lexer from mathematical foundations (regular languages, finite automata) to production-ready implementation. Saturday and Sunday process Stanford Week 3 lectures (parsing introduction) to preview next week's material.

**Two parallel tracks:**
- **M-F:** Hands-on lexer implementation (theory → code)
- **Sat-Sun:** Stanford Week 3 parsing lectures (preparation for Week 5)

---

## Daily Plans

### Monday, January 19, 2026 — Lexical Analysis Foundations

**Topic:** What is lexing and why is it a separate phase?

**Focus:** [[02-lexing/tokens-vs-characters]]

**Goals:**
- Understand tokens vs. characters abstraction
- Learn lexer responsibilities (whitespace, comments, keywords)
- Understand why lexing is separate from parsing
- Identify what information lexer must preserve

**Key Questions:**
- Why not parse directly from characters?
- What information must the lexer preserve?
- How does lexing "remove ambiguity"?

**Deliverables:**
- Training document: "Lexical Analysis Foundations"
- Daily note with chess/AoC analogies
- Zettel: Tokens and lexer concepts

**Duration:** 2-3 hours

---

### Tuesday, January 20, 2026 — Regular Languages + Finite Automata

**Topic:** Mathematical foundations - from theory to execution

**Focus:** [[02-lexing/regular-languages]]

**Goals:**
- Understand regular languages and their limitations
- Learn DFA/NFA execution models
- Understand regex → NFA → DFA pipeline
- Recognize what patterns are NOT regular

**Key Questions:**
- Why can't regex handle nested comments?
- What makes a language "regular"?
- Why does a DFA run in O(n) time?
- What's the cost of NFA → DFA conversion?

**Deliverables:**
- Training document: "Regular Languages and Finite Automata"
- Daily note with pattern examples and DFA traces
- Zettel: Regular languages, DFA/NFA, Thompson's construction

**Duration:** 4-5 hours (theory-heavy, combined old Tue+Wed)

---

### Wednesday, January 21, 2026 — Hand-Written Lexer Implementation

**Topic:** Read → Run → Modify - Complete lexer implementation

**Focus:** [[02-lexing/hand-written-lexer]]

- Implement maximal munch and error handling

**Key Questions:**
- How does lookahead work?
- When does lexer backtrack vs. commit?
- How are positions maintained?
- How to handle escape sequences and unclosed strings?

**Deliverables:**
- Training document: "Hand-Written Lexer Implementation"
- Annotated source code
- Extended lexer with strings, floats, comments
- Daily note with implementation insights
- Test inputs and token streams

**Duration:** 5-6 hours (implementation-heavy, combined old Thu+Fri)
